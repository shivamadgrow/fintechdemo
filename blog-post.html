Reducing-Balance vs. Flat Interest Rate: Why You Must Never Confuse the Two

When searching for personal loans in India, borrowers frequently encounter two entirely distinct interest calculation models: Flat Interest Rate and Reducing-Balance Interest Rate. While a 12% flat rate might sound identical or even cheaper than a 14% reducing rate on surface, the mathematical reality is shockingly different.

What is a Flat Interest Rate?

In a flat rate calculation, interest is charged on the entire initial principal amount across the entire tenure, ignoring the fact that you are actively paying down the loan balance every single month.

For example, on a ₹50,000 personal loan for 1 year at 12% flat rate: Total interest is calculated as ₹50,000 × 12% = ₹6,000. Total repayment = ₹56,000, resulting in an EMI of ₹4,666.

What is a Reducing-Balance Interest Rate?

Under the standard reducing-balance method (mandated by the Reserve Bank of India for all regulated lenders), interest for any given month is calculated only on the outstanding principal balance remaining after deducting the principal portion of your previous monthly payments.

On the exact same ₹50,000 personal loan for 12 months at 14% reducing rate: Monthly EMI is ₹4,490, and total interest paid is only ₹3,884! Even though the headline rate is 14% vs 12%, you save more than ₹2,100 in actual cash because your interest shrinks as the balance diminishes.

Rule of Thumb for Comparing Rates

As a general financial benchmark, an advertised flat interest rate corresponds to roughly 1.8x to 1.9x in equivalent reducing-balance Annual Percentage Rate (APR). A 12% flat loan has an effective APR of approximately 22%!
